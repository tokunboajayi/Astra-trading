It is not a knowledge gap. Women who do invest perform about as well as anyone else. What stops people is the part before the first trade: not wanting to be wrong with real money.

So be wrong here instead. This is a simulator that teaches you to start before you feel ready, because feeling ready is the thing that never arrives.

    Step 3 · about 90 seconds

    A share is a slice of a company.

    That is the whole definition. Everything else in this app is a consequence of it.

    One company, cut into shares

    Buy one share and you own a very small piece of a real business. If the business is worth more later, your slice is worth more. If it is worth less, so is your slice. Nobody is holding your money for you — it has turned into a piece of a company.

    Where the price comes from

    A price is not set by the company, or by an app, or by anyone in charge. It is the number the last buyer and the last seller agreed on. More people wanting in pushes it up. More people wanting out pushes it down. Push it around yourself:

    Step 5 · read the screen before using it

    A line graph is a map, not a verdict.

    Nia's quick tour: time moves left to right, and the price of one share moves up and down.

    The two axes

    A rising line means the last traded price is higher than before. It does not promise what happens next. A steep line means a larger move over that stretch of time, not a better company.

    Market orders, by experience

    A market order asks to buy or sell now at the best available prices. It prioritizes getting filled over knowing the exact price.

    Starting outThink of it as joining the next available trade. The displayed price is a quote, not a guarantee.
    Some experienceSeveral sellers may be waiting at different prices. A large order can take more than one level, so the average fill may differ from the quote.
    ExperiencedThe gap between the quote and fill is slippage. It widens when the market moves quickly or the order is large. A limit order controls price but may not fill.

    Nia's tip: decide how much you can afford to lose before you press Buy. The chart explains movement; it never makes risk disappear.
